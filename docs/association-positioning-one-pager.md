# Ovation for associations — positioning one-pager

*Draft for association-facing conversations (e.g. PCA). Built from the competitive brief and battlecard — see docs/competitive-brief-bettercricket-clubstats.md.*

## Headline

**Your full club history, already there — not another import project.**

## The problem, from an association's chair

Every club in your association wants a modern stats site: player profiles, leaderboards, honour boards, a public record of who's played and what they've achieved. The tools on the market today solve that going forward — but every one of them makes each club start from zero and rebuild its own history, club by club, season by season, spreadsheet by spreadsheet.

That's backwards. You already have the history. It's sitting in scorecards and results across every club in the competition, for every season it's been run. The association is the one place that history already exists in full — it just hasn't been turned into something clubs and players can use.

## What Ovation does differently

Ovation is built on a shared, association-wide database, not a club-by-club one. When a club in the association comes on board, its full history — matches, batting and bowling figures, opponents, seasons, players — is already there, because it was never siloed to begin with. No CSV upload. No "historical migration" project. No custom quote for old records.

That single structural difference changes what's possible for the whole association, not just one club at a time:

- **Every club's stats site can go live with decades of history from day one**, not a blank slate that fills in over the coming seasons.
- **A player's career follows them across clubs.** A junior who moves up through three clubs in the association, or a player who transfers mid-career, gets one connected record — not three disconnected profiles on three different platforms.
- **Club history survives club history.** Amalgamations, renames, and clubs that have changed grade or folded and reformed over the decades stay connected in the record, instead of becoming a gap.
- **The association gets a single, consistent picture** — premierships, ladders, all-time records — across every club and grade, not fragmented across whatever tool each club happened to pick.

## What each club still owns

The shared database is the foundation, not the whole product. Each club still gets its own branded home for the things that make it a club, not just a competitor: honour boards, life members, awards, caps, committee history, and the stories behind the numbers. That curated layer stays entirely the club's own — Ovation just means they're not rebuilding the stats underneath it from scratch first.

## Why now

Every club in the association is already being courted by single-club tools that ask them to start their history over. The longer clubs sign up to those platforms individually, the harder it becomes to bring the association's history together later — each club has sunk cost in a URL its players already use. Standing this up at the association level, once, is both the simpler path for every club and the only way the association ends up with one coherent record instead of a patchwork.

## The ask

A conversation about activating the association's clubs on Ovation together — starting with a small pilot group — so every club's history is live from day one, and the association ends up with a single, connected record of its own competition.
