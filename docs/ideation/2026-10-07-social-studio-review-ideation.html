Reading this review

The ranking is driven by the five complaints, each traced to the code in Owner complaints. Ideas 1–3 replace the scattered settings with one model: per card kind, decide whether it drafts, how it is packaged and when it goes out, then adjust any of that on the drafts themselves. Idea 4 makes design edits stick. Idea 5 rebuilds the screens around the weekly job. Ideas 6–7 are not in your list but are found defects that must be fixed before auto-posting is trusted.

Grounding Context

Codebase Context

  • Surface. One "Social Media Studio" tab bar with nine tabs — Studio, Cards, Create a card, Carousel sets, Fixtures, Junior cards, Trading cards, Queue, Photo library. The Queue (the weekly job) is the 8th tab; the Cards tab stacks ten settings cards; Studio repeats some of them.
  • Render. 6 design packs × 17 card kinds × 4 sizes, rendered by the HTML pack renderer (headless Chromium, server-side). A legacy canvas renderer (share-card.ts) is still used by the card forms, sponsors card and export modal. Client and server pack registries are kept in step by pack-coverage-parity.test.ts; sample-text leakage is linted in pack-lint.test.ts against sample data only.
  • Automation. draft-sweep.ts runs hourly from the PlayHQ runner: drafts results, achievements, round-ups and debut caps from central matches (last 21 days, cap 40), then promotes drafts when auto-post is on (off by default). Round carousels and a perRound weekend wrap already exist.
  • Publishing. Meta publishing (connect, schedule, worker, auto-publish, health) is built but off behind META_PUBLISHING_ENABLED, waiting on App Review; never run against a live Page. Today's path is Preview & download → Share by hand (zip or phone share sheet, caption copied) → Mark posted. Video is a manual MP4 download.
  • Media. Photo pick order: club rule → library photo tagged with the player → headshot → grade photo → no-photo layout. Background removal (Photoroom) is manual, per photo. Juniors never get photos. Captions are fixed templates with name/stat tokens.

Past learnings

  • Earlier owner complaints (Jul 2026 — hard-coded Halls Head, ignored photos, carousels bypassing the pack) traced to two render paths plus opt-in tenant-data threading; fixed in waves, but the structure that produced them remains.
  • Open product decision F3: should a re-ingest overwrite an approved draft? Still unresolved.
  • Deferred in the automation and Meta plans: roles and approval workflow, analytics/insights, Reels/video, Stories auto-publish, weekly posting slots, junior photo consent.
  • No club feedback on Social Studio exists in the repo.

External context

  • Cricket tools (CrickPro) export one-shot result/fixture images; generic schedulers (Buffer, Later, Meta Business Suite) have queues but no match-data awareness; the volunteer status quo is re-keying scores into Canva.
  • The volunteer social admin is unpaid, ad hoc, ideally shared by 2–3 people, and turns over.
  • Community-club engagement guidance (AFL / Sport England via SEN, Sep 2026): people, not logos — milestones, action shots, throwbacks.
  • AP + Automated Insights: automate the long tail humans never cover, with accuracy as the non-negotiable gate; humans spend effort on the one best story.

1. One Posting Plan per card kind

Idea 1 Axis 3 · Automation & content selection Complaints: too many options, packaging, scheduling Confidence 80% Complexity Medium–High
Description
Replace the Automation card, Round cards card, Engines/sizes switches, Auto-post card and Studio's Match-summary auto-draft with one table: one row per card kind (results, milestones, debut caps, team lists, game day, round-up, weekend wrap…). Each row answers the same four questions in plain words: Draft it? (off / on / on for these grades), Package as (each one on its own / one set per round / both / fold into the weekend wrap), Post when (a fixed day and time, or "N hours after the match" / "N hours before"), and Who approves (me / goes automatically after N hours). A row's design is a fifth column linking to idea 4. Global switches shrink to one master "Autopilot on/off". Dependencies disappear: a round set can't be "switched on in Automation above first" because the row is the switch.
Axis
Automation engine & content selection
Basis
  • direct: the Cards tab stacks ten cards (admin-social.tsx:74-83), about 60–70 controls; content engines (admin-social/constants.ts:5-28) overlap automation families (automation-card.tsx:14-20) — Round-up is in both.
  • direct: auto-drafting is configured in three places — Automation, Round cards, and Studio's match-summary-settings.tsx (with the autoseedCarousels toggle).
  • direct: cross-card dependency explained only in copy: "Switch on Round-up / Match day in Automation above first." (round-schedules-card.tsx:140).
  • direct: packaging lives in Round cards modes (Each match / Whole round / Both or Off, :55-83); review window is one global value (auto-post-card.tsx:116); the 48h match-day lead is hard-coded (engines/match-day.ts:8).
  • external: the volunteer admin is unpaid, part-time and changes over — one readable table is learnable; ten interlocking cards are not.
Rationale
This one change answers three of your five complaints at once. The settings already exist, scattered over four cards and two tabs with overlapping vocabulary. Putting them on one row per kind makes "what happens to a milestone card?" answerable at a glance, and adds the per-kind controls (packaging, timing, approval) that are missing today.
Downsides
Settings migration: existing rows in social settings, round schedules and auto-post must map onto the new model without changing any club's behaviour. A wide table needs a stacked phone layout. Advanced knobs (freshness cut-off, sizes, tone) still need a home — an "Advanced" drawer, not a separate card.

2. Package in the queue: combine, split, reorder

Idea 2 Axis 4 · Publishing & distribution Complaint: packaging Confidence 80% Complexity Medium
Description
The Posting Plan sets the default; the queue lets you change your mind after drafting. Tick several drafts and choose Combine into one post (a carousel, with the cover card optional and slide order draggable). On any set, choose Split into separate posts. Cover on/off, grouping and slide order sit on the queue card itself, not buried in the Studio editor, and the queue previews exactly what will post. This also folds the separate Carousel sets page into the queue: a carousel is just a post with several slides.
Axis
Publishing & distribution
Basis
  • direct: the queue (admin-social-queue.tsx) has no multi-select, merge or split; it only reads adjustments.set to show a slide count (:66-71).
  • direct: per-draft set options — "Cover card" Automatic / Always / Never and the men's/women's grouping checkbox — live only in the Studio editor (card-sets/set-options.tsx:20-45, mounted at admin-studio-editor.tsx:265).
  • direct: set size is fixed by planner caps, not admin choice: SET_CAPS = { roundFixtures: 5, weekendWrap: 4, teamListRound: 1 } (lib/scorecard/src/card-sets.ts:213-217); "The set is derived from the card's input, never stored".
  • direct: Carousel sets are a separate system (admin-social-sets.tsx, ~1,365 lines; social-cards-sets.ts) built from source data, 2–10 slides, with autoseed off by default.
Rationale
Packaging is an editorial decision you often only know you want after seeing the week's cards ("these three milestones would be better as one post"). Today that decision is locked in by settings and caps before the cards exist. Moving it into the queue gives direct control and removes a whole page.
Downsides
Sets are currently derived from one draft's input, never stored; combining separate drafts needs a stored "post" grouping above drafts (a schema change and a hand-applied migration). Juniors must never combine with seniors — the merge action has to enforce that.

3. Posting calendar you can drag

Idea 3 Axis 4 · Publishing & distribution Complaint: scheduling Confidence 75% Complexity Medium
Description
A week view of everything that will post: drafted cards sit in their Posting Plan slots, and future cards appear as placeholders ("Results — after Saturday's games"). Drag a card to move it; the change applies to that post only, with "make this the usual time for results" one click away. Club guard rails live here: a daily cap, minimum spacing between posts, and quiet hours. When several cards land in one slot they are spaced out, strongest story first, instead of all posting at the same moment. The same calendar drives Meta auto-publish later and, before then, "post these now" reminders for sharing by hand.
Axis
Publishing & distribution
Basis
  • direct: auto-publish loops candidates with at: now, no orderBy, no spacing or cap (publishing/auto-publish.ts, verified).
  • direct: day/hour control exists only for whole-round modes, in Perth time ("Times are Perth time.", round-schedules-card.tsx:128,158-185); "Each match" modes have none.
  • direct: hard-coded timing — MATCH_DAY_LEAD_MS = 48h, ROUND_WINDOW_MS = 7 days, default slots Thu 18:00 / Fri 12:00 / Sun 19:00 (round-schedules.ts:36-41,140).
  • direct: the per-draft schedule panel takes one datetime-local per draft (schedule-panel.tsx:267) — there is no view across drafts; the Meta plan defers "weekly posting slots".
  • external: Buffer, Later and Meta Business Suite all centre on a calendar; Instagram limits API posts per 24h.
Rationale
"Control over scheduling" means seeing and changing when things go out. Today timing is scattered across a hard-coded lead, per-mode selects and a per-draft date picker, with no single place showing the week. A calendar is the familiar model every scheduler uses.
Downsides
Placeholders for not-yet-drafted cards depend on fixtures and team lists being current. A drag-and-drop calendar is real front-end work and needs a usable phone layout (a day list rather than a grid).

4. Edit the design once, for every card of that kind

Idea 4 Axis 2 · Card visual quality Complaint: template editing Confidence 80% Complexity Medium
Description
The Studio editor can already move, restyle and add elements, but only on one card. Make it a template editor: open any card kind ("Milestone"), start from a pack, change it (text, colours, fonts, photo position, add your own layers, hide pack elements), then Use for all Milestone cards. Future drafts of that kind render from the club's template; existing drafts offer "apply new design". Edits are made per size with a "same for all sizes" link where layouts allow. Retire the legacy background-template builder on the Cards tab, so there is one design system, and allow a club font upload alongside the five built-in display fonts.
Axis
Card visual quality & render architecture
Basis
  • direct: the editor's adjustment model already supports field overrides, hidden elements, free text/shape/image layers with font, size, colour and per-format geometry, and photo focal point and zoom (lib/pack-render/adjustments.ts:1-80).
  • direct: "Save as template" inserts a row with source: "editor", packId and adjustments but does not set defaultForKinds (social-drafts.ts:455-470); the gallery copy says saved templates "appear under Design it yourself on Create a card" — a manual pick, not a default (admin-social-studio.tsx:305-308).
  • direct: card_templates.defaultForKinds already exists ("a kind appears in at most one template", schema/social_cards.ts:142-190), so the schema hook for "default for this kind" is in place.
  • direct: two template systems: legacy canvas card-template-builder.tsx mounted as <TemplatesCard /> on the Cards tab, and editor templates; older "layers" templates are delete-only and retired. The style panel offers 5 fonts (style-panel.tsx:7-13).
Rationale
Most of the editing power exists; the gap is that it doesn't carry over to the next card. Every automated card arrives in the stock pack design, so a club's customisations are lost every week. Wiring the editor to defaultForKinds turns "limited editing" into "your own designs on autopilot".
Downsides
The automated render path (draft-enrich and the server-side render) must apply tenant adjustments for every kind. A freely edited layout can break when the data differs (a long name, no photo), which makes idea 7's checks more important. Per-size editing multiplies effort unless sizes share layouts sensibly.

80%

5. Inbox-first Studio with three areas

Idea 5 Axis 1 · Admin flow & IA Complaints: poor UI/UX, too many options Confidence 75% Complexity Medium
Description
Nine tabs become three. Inbox is the home page: the week's drafts with approve, package (idea 2), schedule (idea 3), share and mark-posted inline, plus "needs attention" from idea 6. Its phone layout shows one card at a time with the share sheet and caption ready. Designs holds the per-kind templates (idea 4), the photo library, and the one-off maker (Create a card, Fixtures, Trading cards as modes; junior cards stay walled off). Plan holds the Posting Plan (idea 1), the calendar (idea 3), connections and sponsors. Every setting appears once, and each draft links to the row that produced it ("why didn't this auto-post?").
Axis
Admin operating flow & information architecture
Basis
  • direct: lib/admin-nav.ts lists nine social tabs, with Queue 8th (verified).
  • direct: duplication across surfaces: sponsors toggled in the Sponsors card, in settings-card.tsx:118-129 and in the modal; sizes switched on in settings and again as modal preview tabs; platform chosen in caption tabs and again in the schedule panel.
  • direct: the empty queue sends people to /admin/social/cards#automation; the approve path spans drawer buttons, a Publish panel and Share by hand.
  • direct: the phone share path already exists (post-pack-button.tsx, navigator.share with the caption on the clipboard).
Rationale
Ideas 1–4 remove settings and add direct controls; this is the shell that makes the result feel like one product. It is the change a volunteer notices first, and it removes most of the "where do I go?" load.
Downsides
A broad UI change across large pages; deep links and admin muscle memory need a redirect map. It works best after ideas 1–4 have reduced what has to be shown; done first, it would only rearrange the clutter.

6. Trustworthy autopilot

Idea 6 Axis 3 · Automation & content selection Found in review (not on your list) Confidence 85% Complexity Medium
Description
Two defects to fix before automatic posting is trusted. Approval is a promise: when a re-import changes a Ready card's numbers, the card goes back to review with the change highlighted ("102* → 98 — century withdrawn") and loses auto-publish eligibility until re-approved; a withdrawn event leaves a visible "retracted" entry. Visible sweep: each sweep records each stage's outcome and skip reasons, a failed stage stops the watermark advancing, and a "coverage" check lists card-worthy events with no draft ("2 milestones missed — draft now?"). Both feed the Inbox's "needs attention".
Axis
Automation engine & content selection
Basis
  • direct: a changed unposted draft takes the refresh path in draft-upsert.ts (~355–395), which updates card data, caption and photo but not status or autoReadyAt; autoReadyAt is set only on insert (:336); the auto-publish candidate is "ready, with auto_ready_at still set" within a 24h freshness window (auto-publish.ts:15,37) (verified).
  • direct: sweep stages log and continue; SweepSummary has no error field; lastSweepAt is written at draft-sweep.ts:224 regardless, and setWatermark(tenantId, hold ?? lastSeenId) at :375 runs after a swallowed failure (verified).
  • direct: open product decision F3 (re-ingest overwriting approved drafts) in the residual review findings.
Rationale
More control (ideas 1–3) means admins will switch automation on. When they do, an auto-approved card whose numbers changed could post unseen, and a failed sweep looks exactly like a quiet weekend. Visible failures and kept promises are what let a volunteer trust the plan they set.
Downsides
Score corrections early in the week bounce more cards back to review; accepting the change must be one tap. The run log needs a small table and a migration applied by hand in production. Holding the watermark needs a give-up rule so a permanently failing stage doesn't retry forever.

7. Preflight every real draft

Idea 7 Axis 2 · Card visual quality Found in review; guards idea 4 Confidence 70% Complexity Medium
Description
Like a print shop's preflight, render each draft from its real data (including the club's own template from idea 4) and check for leftover sample text, a name squeezed below the minimum fit, a photo-led card with no photo, a letterboxed landscape, more than 10 slides, or a private player's name. A failing card can't become Ready or auto-post without a one-click override, and the Inbox says why. When editing a template, a preview strip shows it with awkward real data (longest name, no photo) before saving.
Axis
Card visual quality & render architecture
Basis
  • direct: name-fit.ts defines MIN_FIT = 0.6 but nothing gates a draft on it; pack-lint.test.ts checks sample-text leakage only against test samples (verified).
  • direct: ensurePackTemplates "fails SILENTLY … produces no error, no log" (design-packs.ts).
  • direct: past learning — layout collapse without a photo was caught only by rendering a real PNG; the Meta runbook's "Meta allows at most 10" failure surfaces only at publish time.
Rationale
Freely editable templates (idea 4) multiply the ways a card can break with real data. Preflight is what makes "edit once, use for every card" safe to switch on.
Downsides
Server-side renders are serialised Chromium work; checking every draft adds sweep load. Heuristics will raise false alarms at first.
